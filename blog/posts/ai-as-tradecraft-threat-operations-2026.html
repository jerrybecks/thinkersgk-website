AI as Tradecraft: How Threat Actors Power Cyberattacks in 2026

By Thinkers GK Team on March 26th, 2026

As enterprises increasingly integrate artificial intelligence to improve efficiency and productivity, threat actors are adopting the same technologies as operational enablers. According to Microsoft Threat Intelligence, malicious use of AI has grown significantly, with attackers embedding AI into their workflows to increase the speed, scale, and resilience of cyber operations. For Japanese businesses, understanding this evolving threat landscape is essential for maintaining robust cybersecurity defenses in 2026.

How Threat Actors Operationalize AI

Microsoft's research reveals that most malicious use of AI today centers on using language models for producing text, code, or media. Threat actors leverage generative AI for various purposes including drafting sophisticated phishing lures, translating content across multiple languages, summarizing stolen data for quick analysis, generating or debugging malware, and scaffolding scripts or infrastructure. AI functions as a force multiplier that reduces technical friction and accelerates execution, while human operators retain control over objectives, targeting, and deployment decisions.

The Rise of Agentic AI Threats

Beyond traditional AI-assisted attacks, security experts warn that 2026 will see the emergence of agentic AI as a major threat vector. Agentic AI refers to autonomous AI systems that can make decisions and take actions without constant human oversight. According to Barracuda Networks, agentic AI is emerging as one of the most dangerous cybersecurity threats of 2026, enabling autonomous, adaptive attacks that operate without constant human control. Forrester predicts that an agentic AI deployment will cause a public breach and lead to employee dismissals, marking a significant shift in the threat landscape.

What Japanese Businesses Need to Know

For organizations operating in Japan, the AI-powered threat landscape presents unique challenges. The increasing sophistication of AI-generated attacks means that traditional security measures may no longer be sufficient. Japanese businesses should focus on implementing advanced threat detection systems, enhancing employee awareness training to recognize AI-generated phishing attempts, and working with managed security service providers who understand the evolving threat landscape. Partnering with an IT support provider experienced in cybersecurity can help businesses stay ahead of these emerging threats.

Key Defense Strategies

To defend against AI-powered attacks, Japanese businesses should consider the following approaches: First, implement AI-powered security solutions that can detect and respond to automated threats in real-time. Second, strengthen identity and access management with zero trust principles to prevent lateral movement. Third, conduct regular security assessments and penetration testing to identify vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them. Fourth, establish incident response plans specifically designed for AI-assisted attack scenarios. Fifth, invest in cybersecurity training programs that teach employees how to recognize increasingly sophisticated social engineering attempts.

Conclusion: Preparing for the AI Threat Landscape

The operationalization of AI by threat actors represents a fundamental shift in the cybersecurity landscape. As attackers continue to leverage AI to increase the speed, scale, and sophistication of their operations, Japanese businesses must adapt their defense strategies accordingly. The key is to stay informed about emerging threats, implement advanced security measures, and work with experienced IT partners who can provide the expertise needed to navigate this evolving landscape.
