Understanding Japan's APPI: An Overview for Foreign Businesses in 2026

By Thinkers GK Team on March 19, 2026

For businesses handling personal information in Japan, APPI is an important part of privacy and IT planning. This overview introduces key issues to discuss with your privacy or legal adviser.

This article provides general information, not legal advice or a guarantee of compliance. Requirements depend on the facts and applicable rules. Check current guidance from Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC) before making decisions.

What is APPI?

APPI is Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information. Its requirements distinguish personal information, personal data, and retained personal data. A business should identify which categories it handles before deciding which obligations apply.

APPI does not require consent for every use of personal information. Consent requirements depend on the activity, including certain uses beyond the stated purpose and provision to third parties, with statutory exceptions. A GDPR policy alone does not establish APPI compliance.

Key APPI Requirements to Review

1. Purpose of Use

Specify purposes as concretely as possible. Generally, notify the individual or publish the purpose promptly after acquisition; direct written collection generally requires prior disclosure. Use beyond the permitted purpose generally needs consent unless an exception applies. Check the rules on purpose changes.

2. Individual Rights

Individuals can request disclosure of retained personal data and, where legal conditions are met, correction, erasure, suspension of use, or cessation of third-party provision. These are not unconditional deletion rights. Establish a process for assessing and responding to requests without delay where required.

3. Cross-Border Transfers

Providing personal data to a third party abroad generally requires prior consent to the foreign transfer, supported by prescribed information about the destination and recipient. Other routes include a PPC-designated jurisdiction or a recipient maintaining a qualifying system for equivalent protective measures; statutory exceptions may also apply. These routes have conditions and do not remove other applicable duties. The equivalent-measures route includes ongoing checks and information provision obligations.

When reviewing cloud services, assess the actual data flow, recipient access, contracts, and protective measures. The supplier's headquarters alone does not determine the legal analysis.

4. Breach Reporting and Notification

PPC reporting and notification to affected individuals apply to specified categories of actual or suspected incidents, not automatically to every breach. Categories include incidents involving special care-required personal information, potential financial harm, an improper purpose, or more than 1,000 individuals, subject to the applicable rules and exceptions. Reportable incidents require prompt initial reporting and a final report generally within 30 days of becoming aware of the incident, or 60 days for the improper-purpose category. Prepare an incident response process and consult the PPC's current guidance promptly.

Practical Review Points for International Businesses

  • Map the information you collect, who can access it, and where it is stored or shared.
  • Review Japan-specific requirements alongside any GDPR or other privacy obligations.
  • Assess website analytics and cookies separately. Avoid assuming that one consent banner resolves every privacy requirement.
